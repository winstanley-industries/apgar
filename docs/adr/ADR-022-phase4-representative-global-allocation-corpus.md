# ADR-022: Phase 4 Representative Global-Allocation Corpus

**Status:** Accepted for the eighth Phase 4 vertical slice
**Date:** July 20, 2026
**Applies to:** Synthetic multi-net evidence families, fixed-query controls,
and the bounded thousands-net stress ladder

## Context

The authenticated imported seed proves multi-net Board IR ancestry and exact
ownership, but its two nets cannot support the representative-scale or
family-level decision required by Section 29.2. Repeating many policies for
one request would measure candidate-query shape while evading the distinct-net
contract. Eagerly preparing every proposed large case would also make corpus
enumeration itself an uncontrolled memory experiment because each authentic
workload net owns a compiled view.

The evidence families must be allocator-stressing without encoding a desired
winner in hand-authored candidate geometry. Candidate generation remains a
later slice and must use production CPU A*, exact admission, and the shared
candidate store.

## Decision

- Adopt `schemas/benchmark/phase4_representative_corpus_v1.md` and its frozen
  42-case descriptor roster.
- Use three seeded synthetic families. Portal and fragmented cases repeat
  disclosed two-net bottleneck motifs. Pin-field cases use one board-wide
  portal ladder: flexible base routes consume adjacent rungs, constrained
  routes consume one rung, and longer private rails provide an independently
  checked edge-disjoint assignment. The base incidence chain produces a
  connected many-net conflict graph without an unavoidable common edge.
  Alternative sparse channels,
  orientation, fragmentation, rule buckets, reachability, and ROI vary by
  family and seed.
- Retain three six-net exact cases, six separate calibration cases, and eight
  held-out instances per family. Held-out identity is fixed before allocator
  evidence is observed.
- Make every materialized request an authentic, distinct Board IR net with
  distinct terminal identities, coordinates, and prepared workload context.
- Retain the imported fixture as a non-regression guardrail, not a synthetic
  performance family.
- Freeze realistic `4/8/16` schedules and the fixed-query controls `1x1024`,
  `1024x1`, `256x4`, `128x8`, and `64x16`.
- Predeclare a `1024/2048/4096` distinct-net stress ladder at pool size `4`.
  Materialize one case at a time under explicit net, entity, active-region,
  compiled-node, and logical-host-byte bounds. Any lower maximum is retained as
  a scalability result with its limiting bound, required and configured work,
  maximum preparable prefix, and first unpreparable net.
- Use only integer coordinates and a counter-keyed stable hash for variation.
  Synthetic point terminals do not pretend to be physical pad copper;
  imported physical pads retain owner-aware obstacles.
- Bind each result to the descriptor, normalized Board, prepared workload,
  binary capacity state, and declared portal roster with a stable case
  checksum.
- Mark only the board-wide pin-field family globally coupled. Later Phase 4
  evidence may not derive both required family wins solely from decomposable
  repeated-motif families; a success decision must include a qualified
  pin-field result.

## Consequences

The repository now has an exact-small tier, multiple independently seeded
allocator-stressing families, real hundreds-net workloads, fixed-query-shape
controls, an imported guardrail, and an honest thousands-net attempt contract.
The disclosed local motifs and board-wide portal ladder make their different
capacity pressures explainable while leaving candidate paths to production
generation.

The schema does not assert that every requested large tier fits the current
per-net compiled-view representation. It also does not establish candidate
yield, diversity, allocator convergence, global improvement, timing, GPU
behavior, combined-route exact legality, or Phase 4 completion. Those claims
require the reviewed candidate-session, baseline, contender, frozen evidence,
and canonical-decision slices that follow.
